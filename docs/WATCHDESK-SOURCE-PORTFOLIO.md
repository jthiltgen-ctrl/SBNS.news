# SBNS Watchdesk Source Portfolio

Status: active source-governance record
Effective: October 2026 editorial-production sprint
Purpose: broaden discovery without confusing discovery value with evidentiary authority.

## Core rule

Watchdesk should listen more widely than SBNS ultimately publishes.

A source can be excellent for **early discovery** while still requiring primary records, corroboration, institutional response, or further reporting before any public claim is made.

Source reputation, source class, and claim-specific authority are separate concepts.

The static source registry is a **seed portfolio, not a whitelist of the only places SBNS is allowed to discover stories**. Ordinary story intake should be able to expand what Watchdesk learns about, while scheduled monitoring remains separately governed.

## Portfolio design

The source mix should contain several distinct layers.

### 1. Formal oversight and primary institutional records

Examples:

- GAO;
- agency Inspectors General;
- state auditors;
- courts, regulators, boards, commissions, and official public records;
- U.S. Office of Special Counsel public releases.

Strength:

- strong provenance for what the issuing body found, did, filed, decided, or reported.

Limitation:

- often arrives after a problem has already matured into an audit, investigation, enforcement action, or formal disclosure.

These sources remain essential for verification but should not define the full discovery aperture.

### 2. Independent investigative and public-service reporting

Active examples in the October 2026 cohort:

- ProPublica;
- KCRG i9 Investigations;
- POGO Investigates;
- KFF Health News;
- The Marshall Project investigations.

Vetted but currently disabled adapter candidates:

- Iowa Capital Dispatch;
- Investigate Midwest.

Potential later additions after bounded source validation may include:

- Iowa Public Radio state-government and public-service reporting;
- additional national nonprofit or specialist investigative desks;
- established local/state investigative desks;
- topic-specific newsrooms with transparent standards and stable public access.

A source may also enter consideration organically after one or more ordinary editorial intakes from that hostname prove useful.

Strength:

- can surface stakeholder burden, implementation problems, recurring dysfunction, public-record discoveries, local consequences, and system-design issues earlier than formal watchdog reports.

Limitation:

- even strong reporting is not automatically the primary authority for every underlying claim.

The story should retain links to the underlying record when the reporting identifies one.

### 3. Public whistleblower and protected-disclosure signals

Initial channel:

- U.S. Office of Special Counsel public whistleblower/accountability releases.

Potential later discovery-only channels after technical and editorial validation:

- Government Accountability Project public disclosures and client announcements;
- National Whistleblower Center public news;
- public court filings, oversight letters, filed complaints, or other attributable public records generated by whistleblower activity.

These are **public-source discovery signals only**.

A whistleblower allegation is not converted into a verified fact merely because a reputable advocacy group, attorney, legislator, or government office makes the disclosure public.

Watchdesk should preserve:

- who is making the allegation or disclosure;
- whether the source is the whistleblower, representative, attorney, advocacy organization, government office, court filing, or reporter;
- what supporting record is public;
- whether an investigation has begun;
- whether findings exist;
- what remains allegation or otherwise unresolved.

### 4. Local institutional/public-record signals

Examples may eventually include:

- city and county agendas and minutes;
- school-board records;
- public authorities and commissions;
- procurement/contract notices;
- public audit reports;
- regulator enforcement and inspection pages;
- public court filings where technically and legally appropriate.

These can surface change before conventional reporting reaches it.

Do not add them simply because they are available. Prefer sources with a realistic relationship to the SBNS editorial aperture and a stable bounded retrieval path.

## Organic source learning

Watchdesk should be able to learn from valid editorial work without allowing arbitrary submitted domains to self-enroll.

The October source-learning contract is:

1. a public HTTP/HTTPS story URL enters the ordinary intake system through an authorized path;
2. the intake is analyzed under the normal retrieval, evidence, and semantic controls;
3. the canonical source hostname is recorded as an observation;
4. if the analysis is not REJECT and the normalized source is verified or verified with qualification, that observation counts as qualifying;
5. the hostname becomes or remains a learned **source candidate**;
6. the Newsroom presents its representative story, intake origins, observation count, qualifying count, and status;
7. only the human editor can configure and approve a public feed/listing URL for recurring monitoring;
8. approved learned sources join future Watchdesk scans under the same timeout, size, host, path, content-type, dedupe, and source-health controls as seed sources.

This creates three different authorities:

- **one-off intake authority** — permission to retrieve/analyze the submitted public story URL;
- **source-learning authority** — permission to remember that the hostname produced useful editorial material;
- **monitoring authority** — separate human approval to fetch a bounded public listing/feed repeatedly.

The first two do not imply the third.

### Intake paths

The learning rule is origin-agnostic after an authorized intake exists. Existing durable origins include:

- editor;
- visitor;
- monitor;
- discovery.

A future email-to-intake bridge, public suggestion form, or other approved intake mechanism can participate without creating a new source-learning standard.

Email itself is not currently a Watchdesk transport, and a source is not learned merely because a message arrived. The message/story must enter the ordinary governed intake path.

### Direct source suggestion

The Newsroom may also accept a source/site link for human consideration. A direct source suggestion should create an observed source candidate or seed a one-off editorial intake; it must not immediately activate scheduled retrieval.

Monitoring configuration still requires:

- same-host public listing/feed URL;
- explicit source class and jurisdiction;
- supported adapter;
- narrow allowed host and path scope;
- ordinary public accessibility;
- explicit human approval.

## What Watchdesk must not ingest

Watchdesk is not a Secure Source system.

Do not route into Watchdesk:

- confidential tips;
- unpublished whistleblower material;
- anonymous source communications;
- private email;
- leaked records that require source protection;
- credentials or gated systems;
- material whose acquisition depends on evading access controls;
- private identifying information merely because automation can collect it.

Future Secure Source / GlobaLeaks material remains a separate human-governed workflow and must not automatically enter AI or Watchdesk.

## Source authority model

For each source, distinguish:

**Discovery value**
How useful is this source for surfacing an issue early?

**Claim authority**
For which exact propositions is the source authoritative?

**Independence**
Is the source reporting on another institution, speaking for itself, advocating a position, representing a whistleblower, or issuing an official finding?

**Maturity**
Is the material an early allegation, filed complaint, active investigation, preliminary finding, final finding, litigation result, audit, or later retrospective?

**Geographic / stakeholder proximity**
Does the source reveal consequences that national oversight may miss?

A source does not need maximum authority on every dimension to be useful to Watchdesk.

## Current portfolio balance

The October portfolio deliberately combines:

- national formal oversight;
- federal public whistleblower/accountability releases;
- Iowa formal oversight;
- Dubuque/local institutional signals;
- national independent investigative reporting;
- national specialist reporting in health systems and criminal justice;
- Eastern Iowa local investigation;
- organically learned source candidates from ordinary editorial intake.

Two vetted state/regional sources remain registered but disabled until a bounded adapter works cleanly.

This is intended to reduce two opposite risks:

- seeing only problems already translated into formal audit language; and
- expanding so indiscriminately that Watchdesk becomes a noisy general-news crawler.

## Source admission test

Before enabling a new monitored source, confirm:

1. the source is publicly accessible through ordinary requests;
2. the source identity and ownership are clear enough for provenance;
3. its reporting/publication standards or institutional role are reasonably knowable;
4. it produces a meaningful class of SBNS discovery signals;
5. its listing/feed can be retrieved through a bounded stable adapter;
6. the allowed host/path set can be narrowly defined;
7. it does not require bypassing login, paywall, CAPTCHA, robots restrictions, or other access controls;
8. its material can be clearly labeled by source class;
9. adding it does not imply its claims are verified;
10. the added run time/noise remains worth the founder and system burden.

## Portfolio monitoring

During the editorial sprint, measure source usefulness rather than assuming it.

Useful source-level signals include:

- items parsed;
- deterministic rejects;
- discovery leads;
- gap-ready Newsroom intakes;
- editorial-aperture Newsroom intakes;
- duplicate/known items;
- source failures;
- candidates actually opened for human review;
- candidates that later become serious research;
- false-positive/noise patterns.

Do not rank outlets editorially from a tiny sample.

A source may be retained because it occasionally produces a uniquely valuable lead even if its overall conversion rate is low.

A source may be disabled when it repeatedly creates noise, fails technically, duplicates stronger sources, or adds burden without decision value.

## Expansion rule

The portfolio is open-ended but governed.

Use two complementary growth mechanisms:

- **small deliberate cohorts** for sources that clearly fill a coverage gap; and
- **organic source learning** when real editorial intakes repeatedly demonstrate value.

Do not set an arbitrary maximum source count. Instead, use source-level evidence to decide whether a monitor earns its recurring retrieval burden.

Pause, disable, or reject a source when it repeatedly creates noise, duplicates stronger sources, fails technically, cannot be bounded safely, or adds little decision value.

The goal is not comprehensive internet monitoring or a closed list.

The goal is to give the human editor a materially better chance to see the right local, national, specialist, independent, and early-accountability opportunity while it is still worth investigating.
